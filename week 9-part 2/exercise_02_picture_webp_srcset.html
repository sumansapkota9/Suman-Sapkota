WebP vs JPEG

The same photo, served in two formats. The browser picks WebP when it can, and falls back to JPEG when it cannot — that is what the <picture> element is for.

Check it yourself: open the JPEG and the WebP in the Network tab and look at the Size column.

~53 KBJPEG (800 × 400)
~41 KBWebP (800 × 400)

For this photo the WebP is about 12 KB smaller — roughly a fifth less to download, with the same picture on screen. That is not guaranteed for every photo: a WebP converted from an already-compressed JPEG sometimes saves almost nothing. So the rule is simple — measure the real files, do not assume. The srcset above also serves a smaller 400 px version on narrow screens, so a phone never downloads the big one.
